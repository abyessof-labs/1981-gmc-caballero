# Separating the body from the frame

How to lift the body off the chassis on this car: what to disconnect, how to lift and support it,
and how to get the rusted mount bolts out. Written for deciding how to approach the right-rear frame
rail rot, the body mounts, and the floor and rocker work, and as the checklist to follow on the day.

> **Sourcing caveat.** The El Camino and G-body forums (elcaminocentral, gbodyforum, chevelles.com)
> and every other source page were blocked to automated clients during this research. What follows
> comes from **search-engine extracts** of those threads and of general body-on-frame restoration
> material — mostly 1964–87 GM A/G-body, plus some trucks and Jeeps — not from reading the threads
> or a factory manual. The **procedure** is consistent across many sources. Individual **numbers**
> (mount count, bolt size, torque) are single-source and marked as such. Researched October 2026.

## First, decide how far the body has to come off

There are two very different jobs. This car needs the second.

| | **Partial lift** — body raised a few inches on the frame | **Full separation** — chassis rolled out from under the body |
|---|---|---|
| What it gives you | Access to every bushing, the frame-side perches, and the top of the frame rails | The whole underside of the cab floor and bed, and the frame bare on its own |
| What has to be disconnected | Almost nothing. Loosen everything that bridges body and frame so it can stretch an inch or two | Everything in the checklist below |
| Front sheet metal | Stays on | Normally comes off: hood, fenders, radiator support |
| Engine | Stays in | Forum advice is to **pull the engine first**. Leaving it on the frame is possible but needs the body lifted much higher (below) |
| Equipment | Floor jack, lumber, jack stands | Two engine hoists or a lift, tall stands, lots of cribbing, a second person |
| Time | An afternoon to a weekend, mostly fighting bolts | Days to disconnect, and a garage you can lose for months |
| Fits | Body mount bushings ([issue #27](../../../issues/27)); a rear frame rail tail section done body-on ([issue #1](../../../issues/1)) | Rot along the frame or the floor too widespread to reach with the body in place |

**Decided: full separation.** The owner has found rot well beyond the right-rear corner: the cab
floor at the front, middle and rear ([issue #29](../../../issues/29)), a rotted frame crossmember, and
a couple of frame-side body mounts rotted completely off ([issue #28](../../../issues/28)). A floor
that size can't be welded from underneath with the frame in the way, and the frame has to be bare
to decide between repairing it and replacing it. The partial-lift procedure below stays for
reference, and for refitting the body at the end.

Two things about this car make the lift harder than usual. With floor rot in three places, a
rotting rocker and mounts already gone, **the floor and rockers are carrying less than they
should**, so the bracing below isn't optional, and the lift points have to be on sound metal: firewall
or cowl and upper door hinges at the front, tailgate hinge mounts at the rear, not under the floor.
And where frame perches are gone, the body may already be resting on whatever is left, so expect
some mounts to have nothing to unbolt.

## Before anything is unbolted: brace the body

On a body-on-frame car, the **frame is the backbone**, not the body. Once unbolted and lifted, the
body has only the rockers, floor and pillars holding its shape. With rot in those, the body can sag
or twist in the middle, and the doors stop fitting. Fixing that takes skill and money.

**This car is a high risk for it.** The driver-side rocker seam is delaminating
([issue #4](../../../issues/4)), and the driver-side middle body mount is already completely
separated, so that corner may be carrying no load now.

- **Measure first.** Record the door gaps at the top, middle and bottom of each door, front and rear
  edges, and a couple of diagonals across each door opening. If a measurement changes during the
  lift, stop and reassess.
- **Brace both door openings** before loosening a bolt. The minimum is a length of angle iron bolted
  from the upper door hinge bolts to the striker bolt on each side. If the rocker is bad, brace more:
  square tube welded across the opening (a ladder or X shape), and a cross-bar from one door opening
  to the other so the body can't spread. The doors come off for the full lift, so the bracing is
  what holds the shape.
- **Once the body is lifted, do not open the doors.**
- **Lift straight up, evenly.** Not one end at a time by more than an inch or two, and not at an
  angle.

## Getting the mount bolts out

Plan more time for this than for the lift itself.

| | |
|---|---|
| Mount count | One El Camino source says **7 per frame rail, 6 of them bolted, 14 bolts in all**. Single forum source, not verified. Count them under the car |
| Bolt head | **15 mm** per the same source. Bring a full metric and SAE set anyway |
| Nut | **Caged nut inside the body.** The bolt comes up from below and threads into a nut held in a stamped cage welded into the floor or rocker. You only turn the bolt from below |
| Reassembly torque | **44–59 lb-ft**, per a 1986 factory service manual cited on gbodyforum. Forum consensus is **52 lb-ft** for the body mounts and **27 lb-ft for the radiator support mounts**. Confirm against a service manual |

1. **Soak all the bolts for a week before the job.** Penetrating oil every day: Kroil or PB Blaster,
   the two most often recommended. Where a cage nut is reachable from inside the cab or the bed,
   soak it from above too.
2. **Use an impact wrench, starting at low power and stepping it up.** The hammering shocks the
   threads loose. A breaker bar on a seized bolt is the most reliable way to snap it.
3. **If the caged nut spins** (its cage has broken free inside the body), wedge a screwdriver
   between the cage and the nut to stop it turning. If that doesn't hold, you have to get to it from
   the other side: cut an access hole in the floor above the nut, or drill the bolt out. The
   driver-side middle mount on this car is already separated, so expect at least one bad one.
4. **Rounded heads:** twist-socket bolt extractors (Irwin and similar) are what most often saved
   people on stripped body bolts.
5. **Heat** works on a stubborn bolt, but the rubber bushing will burn and the fuel system sits
   right there. **Drop the fuel tank first**, keep an extinguisher in reach, and never heat near the
   tank, filler neck or fuel lines.
6. **Bag and tag every mount** as it comes out: bolt, washers, bushings, sleeve, and any **shims**
   between body and frame. Photograph each location first. Shim stacks set the body height and the
   door and panel gaps, so they go back where they came from.

## Partial lift — replacing mounts with the body on

The forum procedure for a G-body or similar:

1. Soak, brace, and measure as above.
2. **Disconnect anything that would bind if the body rose 1½":** the steering shaft at the rag
   joint, the shift linkage, the parking brake front cable, and the fuel filler hose. Loosen the
   radiator support mounts. Check the exhaust hangers and the wiring at the driver-side rear.
3. **Loosen** all the mount bolts on one side. **Remove** the bolts and lower bushings on the other.
4. Floor jack under a **2×4 or 4×4 laid along the rocker pinch weld** to spread the load, never on
   bare floor sheet metal. Lift that side about **1–1½"**, just enough to pull the sleeves and upper
   bushings.
5. **Put stands or wood blocks under the body before reaching in.** A jack lifts but does not hold.
6. Fit the new bushings, lower, and **snug** the bolts only.
7. Repeat on the other side.
8. Torque everything once every mount is in.

Do one side at a time, not one corner at a time. Lifting a single corner twists the body.

For the right-rear frame rail section, the forum method extends this: drop the fuel tank, remove the
rear body mount bolts, and loosen the rest forward to the front fenders to get enough lift to cut
and weld under the corner.

## Full separation — the disconnect checklist

Everything that crosses from the body (cab, bed, firewall, dash) to the frame (frame, engine,
transmission, axles, fuel tank) has to come apart. Generic list, adapted to this car. It has **no A/C**
([`air-system.md`](air-system.md)), which leaves out the one job that would need a licensed shop.

### Front end — usually off before the lift

| Item | Note |
|---|---|
| **Battery** | Disconnect and remove. It sits at the front of the engine bay, where everything is coming off |
| Hood | Off |
| Front bumper | Off. It is mounted to the frame, but it blocks the fenders |
| **Fenders, inner fenders, radiator support** | Unbolt and lift off as a unit or in pieces. The radiator support has its own two body mounts |
| Radiator, hoses, fan shroud | Drain and remove with the radiator support |
| Headlight, marker and horn wiring | Unplug from the front harness |

### Engine bay to firewall

| Item | Note |
|---|---|
| **Engine harness at the firewall** | Unplug at the bulkhead connector, or the engine and dash end of each circuit, and **label every plug** |
| **Ground straps** | Engine-to-firewall and engine-to-body braids. Easy to miss, and they will tear |
| **Heater hoses** | At the heater core on the firewall. Cap the core |
| **Brake lines** | The master cylinder is on the firewall (body), the lines run along the frame. Disconnect at the master cylinder, or remove the master cylinder with the lines. Cap everything. The rear flex hose ([issue #5](../../../issues/5)) is being replaced anyway |
| **Throttle cable** | Pedal is on the body, carburettor on the engine. Disconnect at the carb |
| Transmission TV / downshift cable | Carb to transmission, frame side to frame side. Leave it, but make sure it isn't clipped to the body |
| Vacuum lines | Any running from the engine to the HVAC controls or the dash |
| Windshield washer pump, wiper motor wiring | Wherever they cross between engine bay and body |

### Under the car

| Item | Note |
|---|---|
| **Steering shaft** | Separate at the **rag joint** at the steering box. Mark the spline position so the wheel goes back on straight |
| **Shift linkage** | Disconnect the column-shift rod at the transmission. Column and dash stay with the body |
| **Speedometer cable** | Unscrew at the transmission, pull back through the firewall |
| **Parking brake front cable** | Unhook at the equalizer and feed it back through the floor. The one item every checklist warns is forgotten until the body is in the air. Being replaced anyway ([issue #26](../../../issues/26)) |
| **Fuel tank** | **Drain and drop it.** Disconnect the filler neck hose at the body, the vent lines, the fuel supply and return at the tank, the **sending unit wire**, and the sender's ground wire to the frame. Required anyway before any welding or heat at the rear |
| **Exhaust** | Unbolt or cut any hanger attached to the body. One pipe on this car is held up with wire (SAAQ `07 / 308`) — cut it. Drop the system if it is in the way of lifting |
| **Rear harness** | The rear lamp harness runs along the body. Unplug wherever it crosses to the frame. One El Camino source lists a connection at the **driver-side rear**. This is the same harness with the open lighting-ground fault, so photograph the grounds before unplugging anything |
| **Rear bumper** | Off. It is frame-mounted, and the bumper brackets are used as rear lift points on some cars |
| Anything else clipped to both | Walk the length of the car and pull on every wire, line and cable before lifting |

### Lighten the body

Optional, but each item makes the lift lighter and the body less likely to flex:

- **Doors off.** Leaves the openings to the bracing.
- **Seats out.** The loose-seat defect (`15 / 333`) probably means pulling them anyway.
- **Glass:** the windshield is due for replacement ([issue #6](../../../issues/6)). Taking it out
  first lightens the cowl, but makes the bracing matter more.
- **Tailgate off.**

## Lifting the body

**Methods that worked, per the El Camino forums:**

| Method | How |
|---|---|
| **Two engine hoists** | The most often recommended for a home garage. One in front, one behind, lifting together, with a second person. **Front:** chain or strong strap at the firewall/cowl, or around the **upper door hinges**. **Rear:** brackets bolted into the **tailgate hinge mounts**, or the tailgate latch points, chained to the rear hoist |
| Jacks and lumber | Floor or bottle jacks under 4×4 or 4×6 timbers laid **across the underside** at the body mount locations. Raise each side a little at a time and crib as you go |
| Steel crossbars | Angle iron welded or bolted across under the body perches just behind the rear axle, as a rear lift point |
| Two-post lift | A shop option. Lift the whole car, set the body on stands or timbers that reach under it from outside the tires, then lower the chassis away |

**Supporting it:**

- The body sits on **timbers laid across tall jack stands set outboard of the wheels**, so the
  chassis can roll out between them. Use pressure-treated 4×6 or larger. Some cars need an extra
  stand under the very back of the bed.
- **Crib in a criss-cross pattern** under the stand bases if you need height. Keep any stack less
  than **three times as tall as it is wide**.
- **Never use cinder blocks or bricks.** They crack and collapse.
- Nothing goes under the body while it hangs on hoists or jacks. Stands or cribbing first.

### Leaving the engine and transmission on the frame

The forums prefer to pull the engine first, because the body has to go higher with it in. If the
engine stays (as you want), roll the chassis **forward**, out of the front of the body:

- With the hood, fenders and radiator support off, nothing on the body is ahead of the engine.
- What the body still has to clear is everything **behind the firewall**: the transmission under
  the tunnel, and the rear tires and axle passing under the cab floor and bed. The **tallest point is
  the top of the rear tires.** Rolling the chassis out on **wheel dollies or skates** with the wheels
  off, or on smaller wheels, cuts the lift height a lot.
- **Measure the actual heights before the day**: top of rear tires, top of the transmission, and the
  underside of the cab floor at the firewall. These clearances are reasoned from how the car is
  built, not taken from a source.

### Reassembly

Reverse order. New bushings with the original shim stacks in place. Lower the body onto the frame
with a long punch or bolt dropped through a hole at each corner to line it up. Snug all bolts, check
the door gaps against your pre-lift measurements, then torque.
