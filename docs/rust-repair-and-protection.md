# Rust repair and protection — bed, underbody, and general technique

Covers the planned bed and underbody work, general rust-repair method, and the product decisions behind both.

## Sequencing (matters)

1. **Body mount repair first** — see [`body-mount-repair.md`](body-mount-repair.md). Don't burn through fresh coating, and get the repair sealed under it.
2. Rust removal and priming
3. Topcoat
4. Bed liner / underbody coating
5. **Degrease the undercarriage before the SAAQ inspection** — the inspector *must refuse* a vehicle too dirty to inspect. See [`saaq-inspection-criteria.md`](saaq-inspection-criteria.md).

## Step 1 — Remove the rust

No single method fits both the bed (flat, accessible) and the underbody/frame (box sections, seams).

| Method | Best for | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Media blasting** (soda or fine glass bead) | Bed panels, floor pan, sheet metal | Fast; strips rust/paint/scale without warping thin panels; best surface for priming | Won't reach pinholes/micro-pitting; messy; must prime same day |
| **Wire wheel / DA sander (36–80 grit)** | Touch-ups, no blasting access | Cheap, no special kit | Slower; harder to fully clean pits |
| **Electrolysis** | Small/medium parts you can submerge | Very thorough even in pits; doesn't harm good metal | 15–20 hrs per batch; impractical for a frame |
| **Chemical remover** (Evapo-Rust) | Bolts, brackets, hardware | Safe on nearby paint/rubber/plastic, no acid | Cost adds up on large parts |
| **Rust converter** (POR-15 Rust Preserver, Eastwood, KBS) | Pits you can't grind or blast — frame seams, box sections | Converts remaining rust to a stable, paintable surface | Not a substitute for removal where you *can* reach |

**Practical split:** media blast the bed tub and removable flat panels; wire wheel + converter on the frame and inaccessible box sections.

## Step 2 — Prime immediately

Bare steel flash-rusts within hours — faster after blasting, which opens the metal's pores. Prime the same day.

- **Two-part epoxy primer** (SPI, Eastwood, PPG) — the right choice on truly bare, clean steel. Bonds chemically, seals completely, chemically and moisture resistant. What body shops use as the barrier coat under any topcoat.
- **POR-15** — better suited to spots where pitting remains after converter treatment than to fully-stripped bare metal (it doesn't adhere as well to smooth clean steel). **Not UV-stable** — it's a sealer, not a topcoat; anything it covers that sees sunlight needs a UV-stable coat over it.

**2K aerosol epoxy primer** (SprayMax, Eastwood, NOVOL, DNA) is a legitimate alternative for smaller areas without a spray gun. These are genuine two-component products — primer and activator in separate chambers, released by a push-button on the base, then mixed by shaking. Same bare-metal adhesion and corrosion resistance, limited pot life once activated.

> Note on terminology: **"2K"** means two-component. It is *not* the same as **"2X"**, which is Rust-Oleum's marketing name for their Ultra Cover consumer spray line (one-part enamel, no activator, no mixing). Rust-Oleum primer + 2X Ultra Cover is a budget rattle-can approach — fine for a cosmetic refresh, a step down in durability from epoxy + urethane for real rust protection.

## Step 3 — Topcoat

Chassis-grade topcoat over the primer before any liner or undercoating: Eastwood Chassis Black, POR-15 Chassis Coat, or similar durable UV-stable urethane/enamel.

## Step 4 — Bed

Plan: **spray-in liner + drop-in rubber mat on top.**

1. **Rhino Linings / Line-X** (professional, ~$400–800, ~4–6 hrs including 24 hr cure) or **U-POL Raptor Liner** (DIY, ~$100–300, needs a compressor) over the primed and topcoated bed. **Herculiner** is the roll-on option with no compressor.
2. **Drop-in rubber bed mat** once cured — wear protection plus a finished factory look, without the moisture-trapping problem a bare drop-in liner has, since the liner underneath is already bonded and sealed.

**Why spray-in over drop-in alone:** drop-in liners trap moisture underneath, rub through paint, and shift. A bonded urethane coating conforms to every contour and seals the metal.

⚠ **The bed floor is already painted glossy black**, and the listing photos show standing water pooling in it — including at the front bulkhead against the cab. Bed drains likely aren't draining. Expect to find filler or patchwork under that paint when stripping, and **clear the bed drains** — on a '78–87 car they leak into the smuggler's box.

Caballero/El Camino-specific bed mats: see [`parts-sources.md`](parts-sources.md).

## Step 5 — Underbody and frame

Different problem from the bed: road spray, salt, and moisture running into seams. A hard, brittle coating is the wrong tool.

| Type | Examples | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lanolin/oil-based** | Fluid Film, Woolwax | Penetrating and self-healing; creeps into seams and re-coats minor scratches | Never fully hardens; reapply every 1–2 years; attracts dust |
| **Wax-based** | Waxoyl | Harder film once cured, still somewhat self-healing | Less penetrating into tight seams |
| **Rubberized undercoating** | generic rattle-can | Cheap, sound deadening | **Avoid** — hardens, cracks, then traps moisture and accelerates rust |
| **Cavity wax (injected)** | Fluid Film / Waxoyl with wand | Reaches inside frame box sections, rockers and seams where hidden rust starts | Needs access/drain holes; can't be inspected after |

**Recommended sequence:** epoxy prime → chassis-black topcoat on visible surfaces → **inject cavity wax into frame box sections and rockers** through existing drain/access holes → spray Fluid Film/Woolwax or Waxoyl over the whole underbody as the renewable final layer. Re-spray every 1–2 years, especially before winter.

## General rust repair technique — cutting out and patching

Distilled from the sources below. Applies to the body mount repair and anything else.

- **Cut ~¼" past the visible rust** into solid metal. Rust always extends further than it looks.
- **Butt weld is the proper joint** — stronger, no overlap to trap moisture. Lap welding is easier and common on floors but creates a moisture trap; on structural pieces, butt weld.
- **MIG set for thin gauge.** Too much heat blows through or warps.
- **Tack, don't run beads.** Tacks around the perimeter, skipping around so heat dissipates. Biggest single difference between a flat repair and a warped one. *(Exception: structural mounts get welded 100% around all edges — see [`body-mount-repair.md`](body-mount-repair.md).)*
- **Centre-punch spot welds before drilling** so the cutter doesn't walk. Drill slowly.
- **Weld-through primer** on any surface that becomes inaccessible after welding.
- **Seam sealer, then coating** once welding is done — then cavity wax and underbody spray on top.
- **Tools that matter:** spot weld cutter for a drill, long-reach panel vise clamps, body hammer and dolly, cutoff wheel and/or right-angle die grinder.
