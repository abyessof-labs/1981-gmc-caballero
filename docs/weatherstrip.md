# Weatherstrip — kit, installation, and adhesive

Covers the door seals, door frame / roof rail seals, and the window seals. This is the job that
addresses the **water intrusion** items in [`known-issues.md`](known-issues.md), and by extension
the rocker and lower-panel rust those items feed.

## The adhesive: there is no blue one

**No major brand makes a blue weatherstrip adhesive.** The blue is almost certainly one of two
things:

1. **3M's blue packaging.** 3M `08001` Super Weatherstrip and Gasket Adhesive ships in a
   **blue-labelled** tube — but the adhesive inside is **yellow**. Blue box, yellow glue.
2. **Permatex Blue** — which is **RTV silicone gasket maker**, a valve-cover and oil-pan product.
   **Wrong product entirely.** It is sensor-safe silicone for formed-in-place gaskets, not a
   contact adhesive for rubber-to-metal, and it will not hold a door seal.

### Use black

| Product | Colour | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **3M `08008` Super Weatherstrip Adhesive** | **Black** | The one to buy — **[NAPA Canada](https://www.napacanada.com/en/p/MMM08008)**, same trip as the butyl tape. Also [Bumper to Bumper](https://www.bumpertobumper.ca/en/product-details/mmm-08008-3m-black-super-weatherstrip-and-gasket-adhesive-08008-black-5-fl-oz-14787-ml) (good QC coverage), [Grainger Canada](https://www.grainger.ca/en/product/ADHESIVE-SUPER-WEATHER-STRIP-147ML/p/MMM08008), [3M Canada's page](https://www.3mcanada.ca/3M/en_CA/p/d/v000087446/) for SKU confirmation |
| 3M `08001` Super Weatherstrip and Gasket Adhesive | Yellow | Same glue, visible squeeze-out. The blue-labelled tube |
| Permatex Super Black Weatherstrip Adhesive `81850` | Black | Equivalent, widely stocked |
| Permatex Weatherstrip Adhesive (yellow) | Yellow | Equivalent to 3M 08001 |

Black is the right call for one plain reason: **black adhesive on black weatherstrip hides the
squeeze-out.** Yellow shows every smear on the door jamb. Yellow was the only option for decades,
which is why older write-ups specify it; there is no performance reason to choose it now.

## The kit

**[Dixie Restoration Depot `DM60338` — 78-87 Super Deluxe Weatherstrip Kit](https://www.dixierestorationdepot.com/Product/Detail.aspx?s=DM60338)**
is the one that matches this request. It covers door, roof and lower/side tailgate weatherstrips,
plus inner and outer door and lower quarter window mouldings with sealing strips.

The listing says adhesive is included but **does not say which** — no brand, part number, colour or
quantity, and the product page could not be opened to check further. Metro Moulded, a major
weatherstrip manufacturer, sells and recommends 3M Black `08008` as their own adhesive (their
`GL-11`), so that is the industry default, but whether Dixie boxes the same thing is unconfirmed.

**Buy a tube of `08008` separately regardless.** A kit tube is typically small and a full car's
worth of door, roof rail and tailgate seals will likely outrun it — and if the included tube turns
out to be yellow or generic, it should be set aside rather than used on visible jambs. Worth asking
Dixie when phoning them about the felt-set overlap below.

Note this **overlaps the window felt set already priced** (Chevelle Depot WFK 1411 78, $332.99 CAD,
in [`reference-links.md`](reference-links.md)). Check the kit contents against that order before
buying both — the mouldings-with-sealing-strips line may make the separate felt set redundant.

Individual door seals are Precision **`DWP 1710 78`** for 78–87. Canadian sources as ever:
Chevelle Depot and Muscle Cars & Classics both carry Precision weatherstrip.

## How the door frame weatherstrip installs

**It is pins first, adhesive second.** The seals are not glued on blind.

The weatherstrip has **moulded plastic pins / buttons** along its length that push into matching
holes in the door flange. Those locate the seal; the adhesive holds the stretches between them.
Some positions also take **two screws at each end**.

⚠ **The classic mistake — the kit contains two similar-looking seals distinguished by pin position:**

| Pin position | Where it goes |
|---|---|
| Pins **in the middle** of the seal | Door ends and bottom |
| Pins **on the ends** of the seal | Roof rail |

Lay both out and identify them before any glue comes out of the tube.

### Method

1. **Strip the old seal and every trace of old adhesive.** Bare, clean, dry metal. Leftover hardened
   adhesive is the single biggest cause of a new seal failing — it holds the new one off the flange.
   Adhesive remover and a plastic scraper, then wipe with solvent.
2. **Work warm.** Cold rubber will not conform. Do it on a warm day, or warm the seal in the sun or
   with a heat gun on low until it is pliable.
3. **Dry-fit the whole seal first.** Push every pin into its hole with no adhesive. Confirm the seal
   is the right one, oriented the right way, and that it reaches the ends without tension.
4. **Do not stretch it to fit.** Stretched rubber relaxes back over the following weeks and leaves
   gaps at the ends — a leak that shows up a month later. Seat the pins and let the seal find its
   own length.
5. **Glue in sections.** Thin coat on **both** surfaces — this is a contact adhesive, not a glue that
   works wet. Let it tack up per the tube, then press home. Do not flood it.
6. **Let it cure before closing the door**, or tape the seal in place while it sets. Closing a door
   on fresh adhesive squashes and displaces the seal.
7. **One door at a time**, so there is always a correct reference to compare against.

## Sequence with the rest of the door work

The beltline weatherstrip and window felts are already inside the combined teardown in
[`door-job-plan.md`](door-job-plan.md). The **door frame and roof rail seals here are outside-the-
door work** and do not need the panel off, so they can be done separately — but doing them in the
same session is sensible, since the whole point is to stop water reaching the rockers.

Order within a session: **inner work first** (motor, felts, vapour barrier, panel on), **then** the
outer door and frame seals, so the door is not being opened and shut on curing adhesive while the
internals are still being fitted.
