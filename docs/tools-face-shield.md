# Face shield — grinding with a half-mask respirator underneath

Which full-face shield to wear for the angle-grinder work (cutting out the rear quarter and rocker
sections, wire-wheeling, dressing the patch welds) **with a P100 half mask underneath it**. The
grinder is in [`tools-angle-grinder.md`](tools-angle-grinder.md), the discs and wire-wheel rules are
in [`tools-grinder-discs.md`](tools-grinder-discs.md), and the respirator itself is tracked in
[issue #24](../../../issues/24).

**Sourcing caveat:** researched October 2026 from manufacturer, distributor and retailer listings.
`3m.com`'s WP-series datasheet would not load (HTTP 503), and Amazon.ca and Home Depot were not
checked. Cobalt Industrial, Canada Welding Supply and DigiKey Canada pages did open, and those prices
are from the live page on 2026-10-12. **No source tested any shield with a specific half mask.**
"Fits over a respirator" is the manufacturer's claim, not a measured clearance. Try it on with your
own mask and filters before relying on it (see [the fit check](#fit-check-before-the-first-cut)).

## Recommendation

**Honeywell Uvex Bionic S8510.** It has a clear anti-fog polycarbonate visor and ratchet suspension,
and is rated ANSI Z87.1+ and CSA Z94.3.

| | |
|---|---|
| Visor | **8-1/2" tall × 16-1/2" wide**, 0.06" polycarbonate, anti-fog hardcoat |
| Rating | ANSI Z87.1+ high impact, CSA Z94.3 (Cobalt and Saftgard listings) |
| Respirator claim | "Comfortably worn with most goggles, eyewear and respirators" (manufacturer copy) |
| Replacement visor | **S8555** clear anti-fog/hardcoat (S8550 uncoated, S8560 shade 3, S8565 shade 5) |
| Replacement suspension | S8595 ratchet; S8590 is the hard-hat adapter |

Why this one for this job:

1. **It is the only shield found whose manufacturer claims respirator compatibility.** Honeywell
   says so in its own copy. It is also the widest visor here, and it wraps around to the sides.
   The wrap gives side coverage as well as room in front. A half mask's filters stick out in front
   of the chin, and that is where a shield runs out of room.
2. **The anti-fog coating comes standard.** A half mask breathes out downward, so warm, wet air
   rises behind the visor. An uncoated visor fogs in a cold Montréal garage, and then you take it
   off to see. With the shield off, the grinder is running next to an unprotected face.
3. **Replacement visors are cheap.** Grinding sparks pit polycarbonate, and that cannot be fixed.
   Plan on replacing the visor (S8555) as a consumable, not the whole shield.

**Alternative: 3M 82783-00000.** It is an H8A ratchet headgear plus a WP96 clear polycarbonate
visor, 9" × 14-1/2" × 0.08". The visor is thicker, and it fits 3M hard hats if that matters later.
**3M makes no respirator-compatibility claim for it**, and the visor is uncoated, so it will fog.
Buy it if the Bionic does not clear your filters, or if the Bionic is out of stock.

**Not for this:** shields with a **chin guard**, such as the Sellstrom 380 series (S38110). The
chin guard closes off the bottom of the visor, and that is the space the respirator's filters need.
The S38110 window is also only 6" tall, and listings disagree on whether it is acetate or
polycarbonate.

## What makes a shield fit over a half mask

Size alone is not enough. Check these three things:

| Factor | What to look for | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Depth at the chin** | A visor that curves away from the face at the bottom. No chin guard | The filters on a half mask stick out 5–7 cm from the face. If the visor hits them, it gets pushed up, and the top of the shield then sits against your forehead |
| **Height** | 8" or more | The visor has to come down past the mask, or debris can come in from below |
| **Headgear** | A ratchet band on the crown that does not cross the mask straps | If the shield's headband sits on the mask's head straps, it pulls the mask off your face. That breaks the seal |

**The filters matter as much as the shield** (inferred from geometry, not tested). For 6000/7500
half masks, the low-profile P100s are the pancake **2091** and **2097**. The hard-cased **7093**,
which issue #24 picks for spark resistance, is deeper. With the shield taking the sparks, a 2091 or
2097 is reasonable here. If the 7093 clears your shield, keep it. If it does not, use pancakes.

## Fit check before the first cut

Do this with the actual mask, filters and shield, at the counter or before the first job:

1. Put on the half mask first and **seal-check it** (palm over the filters, inhale, and the mask
   should pull in against your face).
2. Put the shield on over it. Set the ratchet on the crown, above the mask's head straps.
3. **Look down at a workpiece at bench height**, the way you will stand while grinding. The visor
   must not touch the filters or the mask.
4. **Seal-check again with the shield on.** If the seal fails now, the headgear is pulling on the
   mask. Move the headband, or put the mask's harness in drop-down mode, and repeat.
5. Breathe for a minute and see whether the visor fogs.

## Rules that still apply

- **A face shield is secondary protection.** Under ANSI Z87.1 and CSA Z94.3 it is worn **over
  safety glasses, never instead of them**. A shield can lift, and a wire or a disc fragment can come
  up under it. So: glasses, then the half mask, then the shield. Check during the fit check that
  the glasses do not break the mask's seal across your nose.
- The wire-wheel rules (guard on, one-minute run-in at partial speed, nobody standing in line with
  the wheel) are in [`tools-grinder-discs.md`](tools-grinder-discs.md#rules-for-using-one).
- **Replace a pitted or cracked visor.** It cannot be repaired. Once spark pitting blocks your view,
  you will start tilting the shield up to see.
- **This is not a welding shield.** A clear visor gives no arc protection. Welding still needs the
  helmet, with the half mask under it, per [`welding/safety.md`](welding/safety.md).

## How this changes the respirator plan

Issue #24 (and `docs/tools-respirator.md`, on branch `claude/peaceful-noether-ph1hg8` and not yet
merged to `main`) plans **two masks**:

- a 3M 6800 full facepiece for grinding, because its lens doubles as eye protection
- a 6000-series half mask for welding

A half mask with glasses and this shield over it also covers grinding. That makes it possible to
**buy one mask instead of two.** The trade-off is protection level. Under OSHA's assigned
protection factors, a half mask is **APF 10** and a full facepiece is **APF 50**. On paint that
should be assumed leaded, the full facepiece is the more protective choice. The shield-plus-half-mask
setup is cheaper and only needs one mask. Both setups need the face shield for cut-off and
wire-wheel work: the full-facepiece plan in #24 already calls for a shield over the mask for that.
