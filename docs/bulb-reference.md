# Bulb reference — 1981 GMC Caballero

Every lamp on the car, with the trade number to buy. The Caballero is the GMC twin of the
Chevrolet El Camino, on the GM A/G-body platform shared with the Malibu — so El Camino, Malibu
and Monte Carlo listings of the same year interchange for every bulb below.

**The one thing to get right:** this is a **1981**, which means the **two-headlamp** front end
(one large rectangular lamp per side, 1977–1981). The 1982 restyle switched to **four** small
rectangular lamps. Parts sites catalogue this car as "78–87 El Camino/Caballero" and will happily
sell you the wrong headlamps. Buy 1978–1981-specific, or buy by lamp size.

## Dome lamp — LED conversion

**Electrical spec of the original #1004:** 12.8 V nominal, 0.94 A, 12 W, 15 candlepower, ~130–150
lumens, 200-hour rated life. Glass is B6 shape, 19 mm diameter, 44.5 mm overall length. On a
running engine the system sits at 13.8–14.4 V, so real draw is closer to 1.0 A / 14 W.

An LED replacement of similar output pulls **1–2 W, roughly 0.08–0.15 A** — about a tenth of the
load, and far less heat against a 45-year-old plastic lens.

**The base is the part to get right: BA15d, double contact.** Two pips on the bottom of the base,
not one. This is not the same base as the 1156/1141 exterior bulbs (BA15s, single contact), and a
single-contact LED will not work here. GM used a double-contact bulb because the dome circuit is
**switched on the ground side** — the lamp is hot at all times from the fuse block, and the door
jamb switches complete the circuit to ground. Both sides of the filament therefore run through the
bulb base; there is no chassis ground through the roof to fall back on.

What to buy:

- Search **"1004 LED"** or **"BA15d LED"**. The cross-reference family is 94 / 1004 / 1142 / 1076.
- **Polarity.** A BA15d base seats two ways round, and most LEDs only conduct one way. If it
  doesn't light, pull it and rotate 180° before assuming it's dead. Better: buy one advertised as
  *non-polarized* or *polarity-free* (it has an internal bridge rectifier) and skip the problem.
- **Voltage range.** Take one rated 12–24 V or 9–30 V, not bare "12 V" — charging voltage is 14.4 V
  and cheap fixed-12 V LEDs die early on it.
- **Colour temperature.** 3000–4000 K warm white looks period-correct against a black interior.
  6000 K reads blue and modern, and makes the interior look worse, not brighter.
- **Beam pattern.** Choose an omnidirectional "tower" or multi-face SMD bulb, not a single
  high-power emitter firing one direction — a directional LED puts a bright spot on the lens and
  leaves the cab dark. 150–250 lumens is plenty; chasing 500+ just creates glare in the mirror.

Two things to check before ordering: pull the lens and confirm the car actually still has a 1004
in there (a previous owner may have improvised), and note that because an LED draws so little
current, a marginal ground or a leaky door jamb switch can leave it glowing faintly when it should
be off. That's a switch or ground fault the incandescent bulb was masking, not a bad LED.

## Verify at the socket

The exterior list and the dome/cluster bulbs above are the standard GM A/G-body applications and
are safe to order. The small interior illumination bulbs — glove box, ashtray, lighter, heater
control, radio dial — varied by option package and are the ones most likely to have been swapped
by a previous owner over four decades. Pull one and read the number off the base before ordering a
handful.

The SPID sticker in the glove box (still unread — see
[`purchase-summary.md`](purchase-summary.md)) settles which lamps this car left Doraville with.

## Buying notes

- **Trade numbers are universal.** A "1157" is a 1157 from any brand; there is no GM-specific
  version. Buy Sylvania, Philips or Wagner and skip the concours reproductions.
- **Stock up on 194 and 1157.** Between markers, plate, cluster and tail lamps, those two numbers
  cover most of the car. A 10-pack of each costs less than two dealer-counter bulbs.
- **Sockets fail more often than bulbs on this car.** Corroded contacts and bad grounds are the
  usual cause of a dead lamp here, not a burnt filament. Check for green crust and a clean ground
  before replacing anything. Replacement sockets with pigtails are sold for all positions.
- **If converting to LED:** the thermal flasher will hyper-flash or stop flashing once the turn
  signal load drops. That needs an electronic (LED-compatible) flasher, not a resistor bodge. Also
  check that the brake and tail filaments still read as two distinct brightness levels — cheap
  1157 LEDs often don't.

## Inspection relevance

Lighting is one of the SAAQ mechanical inspection's checked systems, and every lamp above must
work. Two open items in [`known-issues.md`](known-issues.md) land here directly:

- **Reverse lights — untested.** Two 1156 bulbs, but if they're dead the cause may be the
  backup-lamp switch on the transmission or the neutral-safety/shift-indicator cable already
  flagged as unhooked at the column — not the bulbs.
- **Front turn signals.** Confirm both bulbs are amber (1157NA). A previous owner fitting a plain
  white 1157 is one of the most common ways an otherwise sound car fails a lighting check.

Walk the car once with a helper before booking the inspection: headlamps low and high, park,
both turn signals front and rear, brake, reverse, plate lamp, and all four side markers.
