# Door panel & window switch — clips and retainers

Fastener list for pulling and reinstalling the front door trim panels, with the power window
switch hardware called out separately. Everything here is the 1978–87 GM A/G-body platform, which
is shared across El Camino / Caballero / Malibu / Monte Carlo — vendors catalogue almost all of it
under "El Camino," and the Caballero is the same door.

**Two things to confirm on the car before ordering:**

1. **Power or manual windows.** A base Caballero (Z88) is not automatically power-window equipped —
   `A31` is an option. If a door has a crank, skip section B entirely and use the crank clip in
   section E. The glove-box SPID sticker (still unread, see `known-issues.md`) settles it.
2. **Model year of the switch bezel.** 1979 used a one-year-only *metal* switch bezel; 1980 onward
   is the vacuum-metallized chrome *plastic* "stepped" bezel. Repro switch/retainer kits are
   commonly listed as 1981–87 or 1979–87 — a 1980 car takes the plastic-bezel parts, but eyeball
   the existing bezel before buying. This car is a **1981**, so it is squarely in the common range.

## B. Power window switch (this is the specific ask)

**Two different GM mounting systems exist. This car uses the first one.**

**1. Stamped-steel retainer frame (1964–87).** A rectangular frame that mounts into the door panel
opening and surrounds the switch. It carries **tension tabs on both edges — one set grips the door
panel, the other grips the switch** — so the frame alone retains the switch. This is the bright
frame shown in the eBay kit photo, and it is what a 1981 G-body uses.

**2. Two loose spring clips, GM 9722397 (later cars).** Small bent-steel clips, two per switch,
that grip the switch body directly against the panel. **GM's own catalogue fits 9722397 to
1988–2002** — it is the newer system.

So: **you do not need both.** Frame *or* clips, and on this car it is the frame.

Catalogue data on 9722397 genuinely conflicts — Classic Industries lists it "1968–2002," OPGI lists
it under 1978–88 G-body, Dorman's 49272 lists El Camino 1970–79 and 1985–87 but skips 1980–84.
Vendors cast a wide net; GM's fitment is the more specific claim. **Verify on the car** before
spending anything: pull the intact passenger switch and look at the back — a frame around the
switch, or two clips on its sides.

**Worth knowing:** complete reproduction switches (Eckler's / Chevelle Depot / SS396) ship
**assembled with the chrome stepped bezel and the black spring-steel clips already installed**. If
the existing bezel is pitted or the switch is intermittent, buying the complete switch is often
cheaper than buying bezel and retainer separately.

### Candidate: eBay "1981-1988 Monte Carlo El Camino Power Window Switch Replacement Kit Retainers"

Photographed contents: **one 1-button switch + one 2-button switch**, both chrome stepped-bezel
plastic, plus **two bright-metal retainer frames** — the full rectangular frame with bent tabs that
clamps the switch to the trim panel from behind (the frame-style retainer, not the small individual
9722397 clip).

If it is what the photo shows, this **one kit covers the whole car**: 2-button for the driver door,
1-button for the passenger door, and a retainer frame for each — including the 2-button frame, which
is the hard one to buy on its own. Buying it makes the separate switch and retainer lines above
unnecessary, and the loose 9722397 clips unnecessary too.

Check before ordering:

- Does it ship with **both** retainer frames (photo shows two, confirm the listing says so).
- The 1981–88 range fits this car. It would *not* fit a 1979 metal-bezel car.
- It does **not** cover the door lock switch retainer.

### Candidate: eBay "50PCS Door Trim Panel Retainer Clip, Chevrolet/GMC/Oldsmobile/Buick"

White nylon, conical fir-tree stem with the two-wing flange head — the correct **style** for GM door
trim panels of this era, and the same family as Auveco 11796 / GM 3065575 in section A. 50 pieces is
far more than the ~20-24 the two doors need, but at this price that is fine and spares live in the
toolbox.

Check before ordering: **head diameter (~7/8") and stem diameter (~5/16")**, and that the stem style
matches what is in the door now — some GM panels take a round hole, others a slotted/square one, and
generic listings rarely say which. Unbranded nylon is also softer than Auveco; if a batch snaps
going in, fall back to Auveco 11796.

**Neither listing covers:** the pull-strap end caps, the water shield, the beltline felts, the
armrest, or the switch pigtail. Those are sections A, C and D.

## D. Driver door armrest — base and pad (both missing)

On this door the armrest is two pieces: a moulded plastic **base** that screws through the trim
panel into the door inner sheet metal, and a urethane **pad** that mounts on the base. Both are gone
on the left door and the panel board is torn around the footprint, so the board needs repair (or a
replacement lower panel) before the base has anything sound to clamp against.

**Order by interior trim code, not by eye** — the glove-box SPID sticker gives it, and reading it
settles the transmission and axle questions in the same look.

**Donor interchange:** any 78-88 G-body two-door uses the same armrest — Malibu, Monte Carlo,
Cutlass Supreme, Regal, Grand Prix, Bonneville. Searching those widens a yard hunt enormously
compared with searching "Caballero."

**Unverified:** these vendor pages block automated access, so confirm with the seller whether a
given listing is **pad only** or **base + pad** before ordering. That is the distinction most likely
to go wrong buying blind.

## Sourcing notes

- **Canada:** Chevelle Depot (chevelledepot.ca) ships domestically — worth pricing against US
  vendors once duty and brokerage are counted.
- **Fastener-only US sources:** Auveco direct, Fastener Zone, AutoFastenersAndClips, DealerShop —
  these sell the Auveco boxes cheaply if the 50/100-count quantity is acceptable.
- **Restoration vendors** (small quantities, correct-for-the-car packaging): OPGI, Dixie
  Restoration Depot, SS396 / Ground Up, Eckler's / El Camino Store, Classic Industries, OER,
  GBodyParts, Chicago Muscle Car Parts.
- The authoritative fastener callouts are in the **1981 Chevrolet/GMC Chassis & Body service
  manual**, section on door trim panel removal — worth having before the panel comes off.
