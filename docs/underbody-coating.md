# Underbody rust converter / encapsulator — where it must not go

Product under consideration: a **2-in-1 rust converter and metal primer**, 35 oz black, brush-applied,
thin coats, minimum two, 24 h dry. Same class as POR-15 and Eastwood Rust Encapsulator.

## The caveat that matters most on this car

**A rust converter is for surface rust. It does not restore metal and it is not a repair.**

It converts iron oxide to a stable compound and seals it. Over **perforation, or over flaking
scale**, it fails — it is bonded to loose material, and there is nothing underneath. The driver's
**rocker seam is structural** and, per [`rust-repair-inspection.md`](rust-repair-inspection.md), a
cosmetic coating over structural rot is not an accepted repair and is exactly what an inspector
cleaning back scale is looking for.

**Sequence: cut and weld first, coat afterwards.** Coating an area that is going to be welded is
wasted work — it burns, off-gasses, contaminates the weld, and has to be ground back off. The paint
is the last step of the rust job, not the first.

## Never apply it to

| Area | Why |
|---|---|
| **Exhaust — manifolds, crossover, pipes, cat, muffler, heat shields** | Not exhaust-temperature rated whatever "heat resistance" claims the listing makes. It will burn, smoke and fail. Use proper high-temp header paint. **This car's manifolds and crossover are heavily corroded and will be tempting — resist** |
| **Brake rotors, drums, pads, and caliper slides** | Friction surfaces and moving fits |
| **Brake lines and especially their fittings** | Paint in the threads and flare seats ruins the seal. Brakes are inspection-critical here and there is already a line to replace and a corroded master cylinder — the inspector needs to *see* those lines, not a painted-over line |
| **Rubber — suspension bushings, body mounts, ball joint and tie rod boots, sway bar bushings** | Solvent attacks rubber, and it will not stay put on a flexing surface |
| **Driveshaft** | It is balanced. Heavy uneven coating can upset that |
| **Engine block** | Wrong product, and heat |
| **Sliding and articulating joints** — slip yoke, U-joints, adjusters | Gums them up |
| **Threads on anything adjustable** — alignment cams, adjuster bolts | Locks them and makes future work miserable |

## Two things people forget

**1. Ground straps and grounding points.** Paint is an insulator. Coating a ground strap end or the
bare-metal pad it bolts to creates intermittent electrical faults that are maddening to trace later.
This car already has electrical work pending — mask every ground point and every strap terminal.

**2. Stamped identification.** Do not obscure the **frame VIN stamping**, the body tag, or any
stamped ID.

This one is specific and important here: the photographed VIN
(`1GTCW80H5BD502462`) **does not validate its check digit**, and
[`purchase-summary.md`](purchase-summary.md) already calls for confirming it against the permit,
dash plate and door jamb. SAAQ registration and the required appraisal both depend on readable
identification. **Confirm and photograph every stamping before any paint goes near the underbody** —
painting over a number you have not yet verified is a self-inflicted paperwork problem.

## Also worth masking

- **Fasteners you will need to remove later.** Burying them in permanent coating makes them
  unserviceable. On a car with this much work ahead, that is a real cost.
- **Fuel line fittings** — same reasoning as the brake lines.
- **Anything you have not inspected yet.** The rear frame rails aft of the axle and the smuggler's
  box floor are both still unphotographed. Coating hides evidence; inspect and document first.

## Prep and product notes

- Needs **clean, degreased** metal, loose rust removed. Converters work on *adhered* surface rust,
  not on scale you can flake off with a thumbnail.
- **Thin coats, minimum two**, 24 h between — per the listing. Thick single coats skin over and stay
  soft underneath.
- **UV.** This class of coating breaks down in sunlight and needs a UV-safe opaque topcoat where it
  is exposed. Most of the underbody is shaded and fine, but **rocker exteriors and lower panel
  edges see sun** — topcoat those.
- It is extremely difficult to remove from skin and tools. Gloves, and expect to throw the brush
  away.

## Where it genuinely earns its place on this car

Cleaned-up floors, frame rails that have been confirmed sound, the smuggler's box floor once the
leak is fixed, inner rocker cavities after repair, and the underside generally — **after** the
welding is done and **after** the water intrusion in
[`door-job-plan.md`](door-job-plan.md) and [`weatherstrip.md`](weatherstrip.md) is stopped.

Sealing rust in while water is still getting in just hides the problem for a season.
