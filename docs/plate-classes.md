# Plate classes — promenade vs. "C" (circulation restreinte / antique)

Question this answers: **can a Quebec "C" plate get the car on the road without passing the SAAQ
mechanical inspection — enough to drive it to the mechanic?**

Short answer: **no, and it does not solve the drive-to-the-shop problem either.** The inspection is
triggered by the car arriving from Ontario, not by which plate class is chosen at the counter. The
legal way to move an unregistered car to a shop is the temporary registration certificate, which is
already available and does not require a C plate.

**Everything below marked "confirm" is unverified.** SAAQ's own pages are the authority and they
are bot-blocked for automated clients, so the details here come from secondary sources. Settle both
open questions in one phone call to SAAQ: **1 800 361-7620** (Montréal **514 873-7620**).

## What the C plate actually is

Quebec has no "collector plate" in the Ontario sense. What exists is a **véhicule routier à
circulation restreinte** — restricted-circulation registration — and the *antique* sub-class of it,
whose plate serial begins with **C**.

| | |
|---|---|
| Eligibility | Road vehicle **30 years old or more**, *kept or restored to its original condition*. A 1981 clears the age test at 45 years. |
| Where it may be driven | **Only on roads posted 70 km/h or less.** No autoroutes. |
| Exception | May use any public road while travelling **as part of a group outing organized by an antique-vehicle club holding a Quebec charter**. |
| Cost | Substantially less than a promenade registration — figures in the ~$85/yr range against ~$320/yr are quoted second-hand. **Confirm on the SAAQ fee page.** |

Two corrections to the premise worth having:

- **It is not a weekends-and-parades restriction.** Quebec restricts by *road type*, not by day of
  the week or season. Weekday driving is fine; the 20 is not.
- **"Restored to original condition" is part of the eligibility test**, not decoration. The
  aftermarket open-element air cleaner in place of the factory assembly (see
  [`known-issues.md`](known-issues.md)) is the kind of thing that sits against that wording. Weight
  unknown — nobody appears to police it hard — but it is not nothing if the car is presented for it.

## Why it does not skip the inspection

The mechanical inspection requirement here attaches to **a vehicle previously registered outside
Quebec being registered in Quebec**. It is an entry condition on the registration, and the plate
class is a choice made *within* that registration — the C plate changes what the car may then be
used for, not what it takes to get it into the system. No source found describes an antique-class
exemption from the out-of-province inspection, and SAAQ's own antique-registration path still runs
through the same file.

**Confirm with SAAQ:** *"J'immatricule au Québec un véhicule de 1981 venant de l'Ontario. Si je
l'immatricule comme véhicule à circulation restreinte (plaque C), est-ce que l'attestation de
vérification mécanique est quand même exigée?"*

Even if the answer came back yes-exempt, the C plate would be the wrong tool for this errand: the
shops in [`reference-links.md`](reference-links.md) are reached from Île-Perrot on Autoroute 20,
which a C plate does not permit. Ninety minutes of ≤70 km/h back roads to Vaudreuil-Dorion is the
alternative.

## What does get it to the mechanic

**Temporary registration certificate**, which exists for exactly this. Two forms:

- **12 hours, free** — issued to move a vehicle to the **nearest** place where it will be inspected
  or repaired. Conditions: the vehicle carries the equipment the Highway Safety Code requires, has
  **no major mechanical defect**, and is insured. "Nearest" is part of the condition, so the
  Vaudreuil or Valleyfield mandataire, not a shop chosen for other reasons.
- **4 days, paid** — the broader version when 12 hours does not fit the plan.

Against this car's open defects, the free 12-hour route is a judgment call, not a formality:
corroded master-cylinder outlet fittings, at least one brake line the seller disclosed as needing
replacement, a cracked windshield in the driver's sweep, and a rear seat-belt anchor that is loose.
The brakes in particular are hard to square with "no major mechanical defect" if anything goes wrong
on the way. **Trailering it to the shop stays the defensible option** — a trailer was already used
to bring the car home, so the cost is known.

## Where the C plate is actually worth considering

After the car has passed the inspection and is registered, as a way to cut the annual registration
cost on a vehicle that is not daily transport. The trade is the road-type restriction, permanently.
Worth revisiting then, alongside the agreed-value collector insurance question — not now, and not as
a way around the inspection.

Two things to check before choosing it at that point: whether the insurer treats a
restricted-circulation registration differently on an agreed-value policy, and whether local club
membership (for the group-outing exception) is worth having.
