# Angle grinder selection — DEWALT, Canadian availability

Research for the sheet-metal work in [`known-issues.md`](known-issues.md): cutting out the perforated
rear quarter section, the rocker panel seam once the screwdriver test confirms it, the eaten lower
panel edge, and grinding the patch welds flat afterward.

**Sourcing caveat:** assembled September 2026 from manufacturer and retailer listings via search.
`dewalt.ca`, `homedepot.ca`, `amazon.ca` and most retailer domains could not be opened directly to
verify, so **confirm price, stock, and the exact feature list on the SKU page before buying.** RPM and
max-watts-out (MWO) figures were consistent across sources; bare-tool weights disagree between
listings (some quote shipping weight) and are marked `≈`.

---

## Recommendation

**DEWALT DCG410VSB** — 20V MAX XR, 4-1/2"–5", variable speed, 1,640 MWO, bare tool.

Three reasons it fits this specific job:

1. **Variable speed is the feature that matters on a 1981 body.** Full 9,000 rpm with a flap disc puts
   heat into 20-gauge sheet fast and oil-cans the panel. Being able to drop to ~5,000–6,000 rpm for
   dressing welds on a quarter panel is worth more than extra watts.
2. **Full power on standard 20V packs.** The DCG416B / DCG409VSB alternatives only reach their rated
   1,550 MWO on a FLEXVOLT battery; the DCG410VSB does it on any 20V MAX pack.
3. **Both anti-kickback systems** (see below), which is the tier that matters when a cut-off wheel
   grabs in thin sheet.

Budget alternative: **DCG408B** (850 MWO, ≈3.55 lb) — same protection suite, lighter, and genuinely
enough for this work. See the power note below.

Worth owning alongside it: a **3" cut-off tool** beats any angle grinder for cutting out spot welds
and panel seams in tight spots (inside the wheel arch, behind the rocker).

## Is 850 MWO enough for body work?

Yes. 850 MWO is roughly a 7–7.5 A corded grinder. Cutting 18–22 gauge sheet with a 0.045"/1 mm wheel
and dressing weld tacks are low-load operations — the motor is not the bottleneck.

What actually limits the small tools:

- **Runtime, not power.** Cutting a quarter panel off chews batteries. Run 5.0 Ah or larger; small
  2.0 Ah packs sag under load and make the tool feel weaker than it is.
- **Heat and warping** — the real enemy. Use flap discs (40–80 grit), not hard type 27 grinding wheels.
- **Access.** A 4-1/2" body is bulky inside a wheel arch. The 4" ATOMIC DCG400B is the compact option.

850 MWO would fall short on sustained removal from 1/4"+ plate, 5–6" wheels, or wire cup brushes over
large areas. None of that is body work.

## Anti-kickback: the two systems

| System | Senses | Response |
|---|---|---|
| KICKBACK BRAKE | Wheel pinch / stall / bind-up (load on the wheel) | Electronic brake at max force, tool shuts off |
| Anti-rotation E-CLUTCH | The *tool body* starting to rotate (inertial) | Cuts the motor in under 1/10 second |

A brake alone reacts to the wheel binding; the clutch catches the case where the grinder itself is
being thrown. Thin sheet grabs cut-off wheels far more readily than thick stock does, so both layers
are worth having here.

### 20V models carrying both

| Model | Wheel | RPM | Max watts out | Switch | Protection as listed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **DCG410VSB** | 4-1/2–5" | Variable → 9,000 | 1,640 | VS paddle | KICKBACK BRAKE + ANTI-ROTATION System |
| **DCG410B** | 4-1/2–5" | 9,000 | 1,590 | Paddle | KICKBACK BRAKE + ANTI-ROTATION System |
| **DCG416B** | 4-1/2–5" | 9,000 | 1,550 (needs FLEXVOLT battery) | Paddle | KICKBACK BRAKE + E-CLUTCH |
| **DCG409VSB** | 4-1/2–5" | ~3,000–9,000 | ~1,550 (needs FLEXVOLT battery) | VS slide | KICKBACK BRAKE + E-CLUTCH |
| **DCG408B** | 4-1/2–5" | 9,000 | 850 | Paddle | KICKBACK BRAKE + E-CLUTCH |

"ANTI-ROTATION System" vs "E-CLUTCH" is largely marketing vintage — both sense the tool body rotating
and cut the motor. Treat these five as one tier and choose on power, switch type, and battery platform.

Everything else in the 20V line (DCG405B, DCG413B, and the ATOMIC DCG400B/DCG402B) is brake-only,
without a separately named rotation-sensing clutch.
