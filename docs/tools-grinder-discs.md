# Grinder discs — wire wheels, cut-off wheels, flap discs

Which consumables to put on the 4-1/2" grinder from [`tools-angle-grinder.md`](tools-angle-grinder.md)
for the rust repair: stripping rust and paint, cutting out the rear quarter and rocker sections, and
dressing the patch welds. Includes the wire-wheel safety rules and the RPM check, since a wheel run
over its speed rating is the main way these things hurt people.

## Are wire wheels on an angle grinder safe?

**Yes — they are a designed use of the tool** (grinder manuals list wire brushing alongside grinding
and cutting), provided the speed rating and the rules below are followed. The hazard specific to
wire wheels is that **bristles fatigue, snap off and leave the wheel like needles.** Weiler's safety
slip puts the danger zone at **50 ft or more** from the brush, for bystanders as well as the
operator. Most wire-wheel injuries are eyes and skin punctured by thrown wire, and wheels that
snatched a sleeve, glove cuff or rag.

### The RPM rule

**The wheel's marked maximum RPM (Maximum Safe Free Speed, MSFS) must be equal to or higher than the
grinder's rated no-load speed** — the number on the grinder's nameplate, not the speed it happens to
run at under load. Never exceed it, not even briefly.

| Grinder | Rated no-load speed | So the wheel must be rated at least |
|---|---|---|
| DEWALT DCG410VSB / DCG410B / DCG408B (cordless picks) | 9,000 rpm | **9,000 rpm** |
| DEWALT DWE402 (corded 4-1/2") | 11,000 rpm | 11,000 rpm |
| DEWALT DWE4011 / DWE4012 (corded compact) | 12,000 rpm | 12,000 rpm |

Typical ratings, for scale:

- **4" knotted wire wheels: 12,500–20,000 rpm.** DW4930 and Weiler 13106V are rated 20,000. Any
  decent 4" wheel clears any 4-1/2" grinder.
- **4" knotted cup brushes are often much lower** — the Weiler 4" knotted cup on Amazon.ca is
  rated **9,000 rpm**. That is fine on the 9,000-rpm DCG410VSB, and **over-speed on an 11,000–12,000
  rpm corded grinder.** Cups are where the rule actually bites.
- **6" wheels are bench-grinder wheels** (e.g. Forney 72758, 6", 9,000 rpm). They do not belong on a
  4-1/2" grinder: too big for the guard and often under-rated for it.
- A variable-speed grinder turned down does not change the rule. The wheel has to survive the tool's
  top speed, because that is where the dial will be one day.

### Rules for using one

From Weiler's ANSI B165.1 safety slip and Brush Research's power-brush safety guide, plus the
grinder manual:

1. **Inspect the wheel before mounting** — no loose, bent or rusty wires, no damaged hub.
2. **Guard on, always.** Position it between the wheel and you.
3. **Run-in:** jog the brush at partial speed for **one minute** before going to full speed, with
   **nobody in front of or in line with it.** A wheel that is going to shed or fly apart usually
   does it here.
4. **Safety glasses *and* a full face shield**, on you and anyone nearby. Glasses alone are not
   enough.
5. **Respirator** for rust and paint dust. Old paint and body solder on a car this age may contain
   lead — see [`welding/safety.md`](welding/safety.md).
6. **Gloves and close-fitting clothing.** No loose sleeves, hoodie strings or rags near the wheel;
   wire wheels grab cloth.
7. **Light pressure.** Pushing harder over-bends the wires, heats them and breaks them — it does
   not clean faster. Want faster? Use a more aggressive wheel (knotted over crimped).
8. **Work edges with the rotation running *off* the edge**, not into it; a knotted wheel that
   catches an edge can throw the part or the grinder.
9. **Retire a wheel that wobbles or keeps shedding wire.**
10. **Mounting:** wire wheels for small grinders are threaded **5/8"-11** and screw straight onto the
    spindle — the DEWALT spindles are 5/8"-11. Remove the outer clamp nut first; check the grinder
    manual on whether the inner flange stays on for threaded accessories.

### Wire wheel vs strip disc on body panels

Knotted carbon-steel wheels are the most aggressive brush: excellent on frame rails, brackets,
seams and anything thick, but they **scar soft sheet and can catch panel edges.** On the face of a
quarter panel or door skin, the purple **Scotch-Brite Clean and Strip** type disc is the usual body-
shop tool: it takes paint and rust off without removing base metal or throwing wire. Its own risk
is **heat** — dwell in one spot and it will warm, and can warp, thin sheet. Keep it moving.

## Cut-off wheels for sheet metal

**Thin is the point.** A **.040"–.045" (1 mm)** wheel cuts 18–22 ga body steel fast with a narrow
kerf and less heat; a 1/8" wheel is for structural steel. Let the wheel do the work — forcing a thin
wheel or twisting it in the cut is how it shatters.

**The guard catch.** Flat **Type 1** cut-off wheels are meant to be run under a **Type 1 guard**
(a closed, two-sided shroud). DEWALT's small grinders ship with the **Type 27** guard only, and
DEWALT's wheel charts pair cutting wheels with the Type 1/41 guard. Two clean ways out:

- **Buy Type 27 (depressed-centre) thin cut-off wheels** — they run under the stock guard. Easiest.
- Or buy **Type 1 wheels plus DEWALT's Type 1 guard** for the grinder. The 4-1/2" guard part number
  for the DCG410 was not confirmed — check the manual's accessory list or at the counter.

## Flap discs for cleaning up welds

**Use flap discs, not hard grinding wheels.** A 1/4" depressed-centre grinding wheel removes metal
fast and dumps heat into 20-ga sheet; flap discs cut cooler, blend flatter and are far more
forgiving. **Zirconia or ceramic, 60 grit to knock the weld down, 80 to blend.**

- **Type 29** (conical) is more aggressive and better on edges and fillets.
- **Type 27** (flat) sits flatter on a panel butt weld and is easier to keep from gouging.
- **Grind the weld, not the panel.** Short passes, move around the patch, let it cool — the same
  heat-warping logic as stitch welding. On the variable-speed grinder, **drop to ~5,000–6,000 rpm**
  for finish blending (see the grinder doc).
