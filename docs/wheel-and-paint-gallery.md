# Wheel and paint gallery — 1978–1987 El Camino and Caballero

Photo sets of 1978–1987 El Caminos and Caballeros, grouped by look: stock, factory two-tone, decal
packages, SS, black cars, period aftermarket wheels, restomod, pro street, and orange paint. Use it
to choose wheels and paint for this car. For forums and build threads, see
[`community-and-inspiration.md`](community-and-inspiration.md).

> **How this was assembled.** In the first pass, the session's network policy blocked every image host and most car
> sites (Wikimedia, Bring a Trailer, classic.com, Barn Finds, the forums), so no photos are stored in
> the repo. Each link below was found by web search and should open normally in a browser. The
> notes on wheels and paint come from search summaries. **Treat them as a guide to what you'll see,
> not as verified specs.** Bring a Trailer was not searchable at all; browse it directly. Listings
> drop off dealer sites once sold, so expect some links to die. Prices are **USD** sale or asking
> prices as reported.

## What the factory fitted

Use this to judge "correct" on a survivor, or to pick a period-correct look.

| | |
|---|---|
| Wheel diameter | **14"** factory on all trims |
| Bolt pattern / hub bore | **5 × 4.75" (5 × 120.65 mm), 70.3 mm bore**. Same as Chevelle, Monte Carlo and Malibu, so any G-body wheel bolts on. |
| Base wheel | Steel wheel with a small hubcap or a full wheel cover |
| Rally wheel (RPO ZJ7) | Styled steel wheel with centre cap and trim ring. Rally wheels on the **Super Sport were painted to match the lower body colour**. |
| Sport wheel covers, gold (55P) | 1978–1982 |
| Wire wheel covers (N95) | 1978–1985; locking wire covers (N91) for 1986–87 |

**Rally wheel cap detail: unconfirmed, the sources conflict.** One source says 1978–79 rallies have
rectangular slots and later ones triangular holes. Another says 1978–81 used a chrome cap with six
square cut-outs, and 1982–87 a black cap with five "house-shaped" holes. If a correct 1981 rally
matters, settle it in the El Camino Central thread
[Factory rally rims 78-81 / 82-87](https://www.elcaminocentral.com/threads/factory-rally-rims-78-81-82-87.247771/)
before buying.

**Factory colours.** The 1981 El Camino list on gtcarlot reads Beige, Black, Champagne, Cream,
Custom Burnt Orange, Custom Luxo Blue Metallic, Dark Blue Metallic, Jade Green Metallic, Light Blue
Metallic, Light Brown Metallic, Light Jade Green Metallic, Light Maroon Metallic, Maroon Metallic,
Medium Brown Metallic, Silver Metallic, and White. **The two "Custom" entries are probably not GM
colours.** That site's lists take user submissions; its 1984 El Camino list includes a Mercedes-Benz
Maybach blue. No orange appears in any of its 1978–87 lists apart from that one entry. So **no
factory orange is confirmed for these years**; see section 9 for what owners have done instead.
It is **inferred** that the 1981 Caballero used the same palette as the El Camino. For codes by
year, see
[gtcarlot — El Camino colours](http://gtcarlot.com/colors/Chevrolet/El+Camino/) and
[hdpaintcode — 1980–89 GM codes](https://hdpaintcode.com/1980-to-1989-gm-paint-codes-1/). The
[1978–1987 El Camino database](https://www.tradecraftspecialties.com/5thGenElky) decodes RPOs.

## 9. Orange, copper and burnt orange

Compiled October 2026 to answer one question: what does a 1978–87 body look like in orange?

> **How this section was assembled.** This pass could open Bring a Trailer, Wikimedia Commons and
> YouTube, and **every car marked "seen" below was checked by eye** for body style and colour. It
> was a full sweep, not a sample: every 1978–87 El Camino and Caballero on Bring a Trailer
> (120 auctions), every 1978–87 photo in Wikimedia Commons (about 160), and about 260 YouTube
> videos from 22 searches. Colour names in quotes are the seller's or video title's words. Rows marked
> **text only** come from listing text: their photos were blocked (Streetside, ClassicCars dealer
> photos, Copart) or the page could not be opened. Flickr's image server refused this session, so
> Flickr is a browse link at the end. Prices are **USD**.

**Orange is rare on these cars.** Wikimedia Commons has no orange 1978–87 car at all. Of 120 Bring
a Trailer auctions, four are orange or copper. Old Cars Data puts orange at about 5% of El Camino
sales across all generations, and most of those are 1968–72 Hugger Orange cars. There is no
confirmed factory orange (see "Factory colours" above), so every orange car below is a repaint.

## Where this leaves our car

Ours is a **single-colour black base Z88**. Whether its current wheels are factory rallies is not
confirmed from the photos (see [`purchase-summary.md`](purchase-summary.md)). The photos above
suggest four ways to go.

1. **Stock black, rally wheels.** Correct for a base 1981, and cheap. Reproduction 14" and 15"
   rally wheels are still made. Pick the cap style only after settling the 78–81 vs 82–87 question
   above.
2. **Black over a second colour.** The 1985 black/red Amarillo on Silodrome and the 1980
   black/root-beer restomod show it working. Amarillo moulding-break placement would be
   period-correct for a Caballero.
3. **Period aftermarket on 15".** Cragar S/S or Torq Thrust on black is the classic 1980s street
   look. 15×7 on a stock-height car is well documented.
4. **Modern 17"–18", lowered.** Size it from the Element Wheels and Custom Wheel Offset galleries,
   which list offsets on real cars.
5. **Orange, in one of three strengths** (section 9). From least to most change: orange stripes on
   black, using the reproduction Sport Decor or SS kits; orange over black at the factory
   moulding line, like the 1985 Gateway car; or a full orange or copper respray, like the 1981
   Gateway Caballero. Orange is not a factory colour for these years, so all three are custom.
