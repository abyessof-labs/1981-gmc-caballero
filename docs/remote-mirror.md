# Driver's door remote mirror control

What the empty hole in the driver's door trim panel is, what part fills it, and — the question that
decides the cost — whether the cable has to come with it.

**Sourcing caveat.** Researched September 2026. Every vendor and forum site in this subject area
(OER, Classic Industries, OPGI, Ground Up/SS396, Dixie Restoration Depot, Chevelle Depot,

## What it is called

The hole in the upper-forward corner of the driver's door trim is where the **remote control
mirror** adjuster lives. It is not electric. On a 1978–88 GM A/G-body it is a **cable-operated
(Bowden cable) remote mirror**: a small joystick knob in the door panel, three thin cables inside a
sheath running forward through the door, and the mirror head at the far end.

The GM option codes to look for on the glove-box SPID sticker (see issue #10):

| RPO | What it is |
|---|---|
| `D33` | Outside mirror, remote control, LH only |
| `D35` | Sport mirrors, dual — LH remote, RH manual |
| `D68` | The code vendor copy quotes for the later (`86–88`) dual remote sport mirror kit |

**Which code applies to a 1981 Caballero is unconfirmed** — `D33` and `D35` are the 1981-era codes
in the RPO lists, `D68` shows up in vendor copy for the mid-80s cars. The sticker settles it.

### The three pieces, and which are sold separately

| Piece | What to search for | Sold separately? |
|---|---|---|
| Mirror head + base + cable + joystick knob | "remote outer door mirror, bullet style, LH, **with cable**" | **No — this is one assembly.** The cables are swaged into the mirror head at the factory |
| The chrome plate in the door panel the joystick pokes through | "remote mirror **inner door bezel**" / escutcheon — GM `20086901` | **Yes**, ~$35–40 reproduction |
| Tiny allen set screw that locks the joystick into the bezel | — | Comes with some bezels; otherwise a hardware-store grub screw |

**So: is it the whole cable system?** It depends on what is actually missing, and only two outcomes
exist:

- **Cable and knob still there, dangling behind the trim** → you need the **bezel only**, ~$35–40.
- **Nothing behind the panel, or the mirror is a non-remote mirror** → you need a **complete LH
  remote mirror assembly**, $140–180 new reproduction, $50–165 used — because **the cable is not
  sold as a service part**. Forum consensus on the G-body and the same design on other GM cars is
  consistent: a broken or missing cable means a new mirror. Cable-end repairs exist (un-swaging the
  crimps, brass tube and silver solder) but they are one-off fabrication, not a parts order.

## Check this before buying anything — three minutes

1. **Look through the hole**, or peel the lower panel back. Is there a joystick knob on the end of a
   black cable sheath, pushed back inside the door? If yes, this is a $35 bezel job — the previous
   owner lost the trim plate and the knob fell in.
2. **Look at the outside mirror base.** A cable-remote mirror has the cable sheath entering the door
   skin at the mirror base, usually through a rubber grommet. A non-remote mirror has a solid base
   and nothing entering the door. If the mirror on the car is non-remote, somebody swapped the
   mirror (or the door), and the whole assembly is needed.
3. **Note which mirror style is on the car.** Two different families: the small **chrome standard**
   mirror (base 1978–81 G-body, OPGI `L241285`) and the larger painted **"bullet"/sport** mirror.
   The purchase photos show **black** mirrors, not chrome and not body colour — consistent with
   painted sport mirrors, but confirm by eye before ordering, because the remote versions are
   catalogued per style.
4. **Check the passenger door and the dash.** If the RH mirror is also remote, its control on an
   81–88 car is a **dash-mounted** bezel, not a door one — knowing what the car has stops a
   wrong-side order.

## The trap: the mirror the Canadian vendors stock is the wrong one

Both Tier-1 Canadian sources in [`parts-sourcing.md`](parts-sourcing.md) —
[**Chevelle Depot**](https://www.chevelledepot.ca/exterior/mirrors/left-hand-mirror/camaro-outside-bullet-door-mirror-remote-left-1970-1981)
and [**Muscle Cars & Classics**](https://www.musclecarsandclassics.ca/exterior/mirrors/left-hand-mirror/camaro-outside-bullet-door-mirror-remote-left-1970-1981)
— list OER [**`9865801`, "1970-81 GM Remote Outer Bullet Mirror, LH"**](https://www.oerparts.com/product/9865801.html)
(~$170–190 list; [Speedway Motors](https://www.speedwaymotors.com/OER-9865801-1970-81-GM-Remote-Outer-Bullet-Mirror-LH,121595.html)
carries it too). It is tempting: right side, right style, cable included, in Canada, and the year
range ends in 1981.

**OER's own fitment for `9865801` does not include the G-body.** It reads: Camaro 70–81, Firebird /
Trans Am 70–81, Nova 73–74, Vega 74–77, Monza 75–78, Astre 75–77, Sunbird 76–78, LeMans/GTO 70–72,
Cutlass/442 **70–72**. The 1978–88 A/G-body mirror is a separate catalogue entry (`MK-870`,
`KM01058`). The overlapping "1981" in the title is a body-style coincidence, not fitment.

Same play as the door panels: ask Chevelle Depot or Muscle Cars & Classics to **order in the
78–88 G-body part**, rather than buying the 1970–81 mirror off the year overlap.

## Fitting it

- **The cable is barely long enough.** Owner reports are consistent that routing is trial and error
  and there is almost no slack — plan the route before committing the panel.
- **Bezel orientation:** mount it with the **set screw pointing toward the passenger side**.
- **Seat the joystick before tightening.** The knob housing has a slot that must engage the switch
  face as the panel goes on; if it is not seated, tightening the tiny allen set screw will not hold
  it and the knob keeps popping loose.
- **Before condemning a sloppy cable:** pull the knob out of the panel and twist it firmly both
  ways. The cables stretch with age and the twist takes up the slack — a documented fix on these.
- **Do it inside the door job.** The panel is already coming off for the window motors, the
  weatherstrip and the vapour barrier — see [`door-job-plan.md`](door-job-plan.md). Fitting a mirror
  cable means pulling the trim panel, so it belongs in that teardown, not as its own afternoon.

## Open question: which mirror does the door bezel control?

Vendor copy disagrees and neither source is a service manual:

- Chicago Muscle Car Parts sells a **dash**-mounted remote mirror bezel for the **RH** mirror,
  81–88 Monte Carlo / El Camino — implying door = LH, dash = RH.
- Dixie's copy for the **door** bezel `DM30125` says it fits the driver's door panel "for cars
  equipped with the remote mirror adjuster for the **passenger** side door."

Both cannot be right. It does not change what to buy — the bezel is the same part either way — but
it changes which mirror moves when the knob does. Settle it on the car by following the cable out of
the door.
