# Chassis parts — floor pans, frame rail, body mounts, parking brake cables

Where to buy the sheet metal, frame and parking-brake parts the 2026-09-10 SAAQ inspection calls
for, and the fitment traps on each. For whoever places the order or walks into the parts counter.
Researched October 2026. Prices are list at time of writing, not quotes.

Relates to [`known-issues.md`](known-issues.md): the right-rear frame rail rot (`09 / 196`), the rear
body mount cushions (`09 / 198`), the separated driver-side middle body mount (not on the report),
the parking brake (`23 / 135`, `33 / 135`, `07 / 158`), the smuggler's box floor, and the loose seats
(`15 / 333`).

## The traps, before any part number

| Trap | What it means for this car |
|---|---|
| **The El Camino frame is its own part.** All 1978–87 El Camino/Caballero frames are the same, and unique to the El Camino — longer wheelbase than the Malibu/Monte Carlo coupes | Rear frame rail sections come in **El Camino** and **"G-body 2dr exc. El Camino"** versions (OPGI `L241348` is the *exc. El Camino* one; Ground Up has `RFR-78GR` for G-body vs `RFR-78ER/EL` for El Camino). **Buy the El Camino listing.** A donor frame has to come from an El Camino or Caballero |
| **"Trunk floor" parts do not apply.** The coupe's rear body mounts sit in the trunk floor extensions; the El Camino has a bed, not a trunk | Skip any *trunk floor extension with body mount brace*, *trunk floor to frame repair plates*, or *under rear seat floor brace (except El Camino)* listing |
| **The front parking brake cable changed in 1981.** Automatic-transmission cars got a different front cable from 1981 | **1978–80 auto: 54¾"**. **1981–87 auto (and all manuals): 56¼"**. This car is a 1981 automatic, so **the 56¼" cable** — and the *1981–88 automatic* kit, not the *1978–80 automatic* one |
| **"Front cable" does not mean front wheels.** | See below — the parking brake works on the **rear wheels only** |

## Parking brake — what "the full assembly" actually is

### How it is laid out

The parking brake on this car acts on the **rear drums only**. The front wheels have disc brakes
with no parking function, so there is nothing to buy for the front wheels. "Front cable" is the
trade name for the *front end of the system* — the cable from the pedal.

The run, front to back:

| Piece | Runs from → to |
|---|---|
| **Front cable** | Parking brake pedal, under the dash, back under the floor to the equalizer/connector |
| **Equalizer** (guide/yoke) | Joins the front cable to the rear cables and balances pull between the two wheels. Carries the adjusting nut |
| **Rear cable(s)** | Equalizer → each rear drum backing plate, where the end hooks the parking brake lever on the rear shoe |
| Clips, nut, guides | Hold the cables to the frame and set the adjustment |

**Three cables is the expected count** — the front, plus one to each rear wheel. Vendors name them
differently: Inline Tube's complete kit is described as *front, intermediate and rear* cables;
parts-store catalogues list *front*, *rear left* and *rear right*. Forum extracts describe the
driver-side rear cable passing through the equalizer and the passenger-side one hooking to it,
which is the same three-cable layout. **Inferred from search extracts, not verified under this car**
— confirm by counting what is there once it is on the hoist.

### Why cables alone may not fix `135`

The inspection failed the parking brake as **ineffective at both rear wheels** (`23 / 135`,
`33 / 135`) *and* separately flagged the cable (`07 / 158`). A seized or frayed cable explains
both, but so does seized hardware *inside* the drums — the parking brake lever on the rear shoe, the
strut bar between the shoes, and self-adjusters that have stopped adjusting. **Pull both rear drums
before ordering**, and plan the job together with the rear flex hose repair ([issue #5](../../../issues/5)), which
opens the same area anyway.

## Rear frame rail — the `09 / 196` repair

What the inspector wrote: the **right rear corner, at the body mount, completely rotted**. The
reproduction parts for exactly that are **rear frame rail tail sections** ("horns") — the section
behind the rear wheel that carries the rearmost body mount perch, which is where these rot. They
come with the perch already on, EDP-coated, and are cut in and butt-welded to sound rail.

| **Classic 2 Current Fabrication** — c2cfabrication.com | 1978–87 El Camino rear frame rail, rear section | Left / right | Not retrieved | Also sells through Amazon |

**Buy the section only after the hoist inspection.** Issue #1 is still open: nobody has seen how far
forward the rot runs. If it stops in the tail, one `RFR-78ER`-type section is the job. If it has
reached the kick-up over the axle or the rear crossmember, a tail section is not enough and the
choice becomes a longer splice or a donor El Camino frame. **Order both sides only if the left
proves bad too** — the inspector did not flag it.

Per [`rust-repair-inspection.md`](rust-repair-inspection.md), a weakening frame repair is its own
SAAQ rejection, so this one wants full-penetration butt welds into clean metal. The forum-reported
method is body-on: drop the fuel tank, remove the rear body mount bolts, loosen the rest forward to
the front fenders to get lift, cut out with a reciprocating saw, fit and butt-weld. Bracing, the mount
bolts, and the full body-off alternative are in [`body-off-frame.md`](body-off-frame.md).

## Body mounts — bushings and brackets

### The bushings (`09 / 198`)

Replace the **whole set**, not just the rear pair the inspector named — the mounts are a system, and
the bolts come out for the frame rail job anyway.

| **OER** — oerparts.com | 22-piece body mount bushing set, 1978–87 GM | Rubber | Factory-spec reproduction |

| **Energy Suspension** | **`3.4141R`** | Polyurethane, red | Listed 1978–87 El Camino; includes radiator support mounts |
| **Dixie** | `DM94019 WITH` — body bushing hardware, with radiator support | Hardware only | Bolts, nuts, washers |
| **Chevelle Depot** | G-body body mount bushing hardware kit; El Camino body mount bolt kit; body mount & core support washer set | Hardware | Canadian stock |

**Rubber over urethane** for a driver: urethane transmits more noise and vibration into the cab
and is chosen for stiffness in performance builds. Not a strong preference — either passes.

### The brackets — two different things

**Frame-side perches** are the stamped brackets welded to the frame rail that the bushing sits in.
The rearmost one comes **already on** the rear frame rail section above, which is the part to buy
for the right-rear corner. Universal weld-on perch-and-bushing kits exist (eBay, *88-145KT*) if a
mid-frame perch is gone — not El Camino-specific; measure before using one.

**Body-side supports** are the reinforcements in the cab floor the body mount bolt threads into.
The **driver-side middle mount** — the one that is completely separated on this car — bolts into a
reinforcement under the driver's door, outboard by the rocker and above the frame rail. Per forum
extracts, the floor is spot-welded to it, the inner rocker is welded to it, and it carries a
**caged nut for the middle body mount and the anchor for the lower driver seat belt bolt.**

That puts three of this car's open items on one piece of sheet metal: the separated mount, the
loose belt anchor ([issue #7](../../../issues/7)), and the rocker seam rot ([issue #4](../../../issues/4)).
**Inspect them as one job.**

| Option | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Reproduction of that reinforcement | **None found** for the El Camino | Seat-mount braces and 3- and 7-piece *A/G-body seat mounting brace* kits exist (eBay; Chevelle Depot RH outer at **$125.99**, assumed CAD) but none was described as the body-mount/belt-anchor piece |
| Repair the hole in place | Fabricate | Forum method: a flat **0.090"–0.120" steel donut** welded over a rusted-out mount hole, with a new caged nut behind it. Replacing the whole reinforcement means drilling spot welds from the floor side or cutting the floor out around it |
| Used | Donor El Camino / Caballero cab floor | Tier 3 salvage — see [`parts-sourcing.md`](parts-sourcing.md) |
