# Instrument cluster bezel — loose trim around the gauges

The trim that surrounds the gauge cluster and stands proud of the dash face. Catalogued as the
**instrument cluster bezel**, also **dash bezel** or **instrument panel trim plate**. Searching for
"trim" or "surround" finds nothing; searching for "bezel" finds everything.

## It is held on by screws, not clips

**Roughly 8 screws hold this bezel to the dash on a 1981.** There are no retaining clips in the
design, so "missing clips" is almost certainly one of two things:

1. **Missing or backed-out screws** — the cheap outcome. Count the holes against the screws present.
2. **Broken screw bosses / mounting tabs** — the likely one. This plastic is 45 years old and has
   spent its life under a windshield. The moulded bosses the screws thread into go brittle and snap,
   so the screw is either gone or spinning in a broken boss. No fastener fixes that on its own.

**Diagnose before buying anything.** Back the screws out, lift the bezel off, and look at the boss
behind each hole. That one look decides between a $5 fix and a hunt for a rare part.

For reference on what else comes apart back there: behind the bezel, 3–4 screws hold the clear dust
cover, 3–4 hold the metal gauge carrier, and 5 hold the housing to the dash.

## The 1981 bezel is close to a one-year part

Two changes bracket it, and this is why the sourcing is hard:

- **1981** is when the cluster changed from the horizontal **"strip" speedometer** to a conventional
  **round dial**. So 1978–80 is a different cluster generation.
- **1982** is when the **wiper switch moved off the dash** onto the turn-signal stalk. The 82–87
  bezels have no wiper switch cutout, so they will not work on this car.

That leaves 1981 needing a bezel with the dash wiper-switch opening *and* the round-dial cluster
aperture. El Camino Central owners report exactly this — that **the '81 panels are the hardest to
find** of the whole 78–87 run.

Caveat worth checking against the actual part before buying: whether a 1978–80 bezel physically
interchanges depends on whether the cluster aperture changed along with the speedometer, which is
not confirmed here. **82–87 is confirmed not to fit.** Treat any listing that says "78-87 fits all"
with suspicion.

## Recommendation: repair, don't replace

Given a near-unobtainable one-year part in brittle plastic, **rebuilding the mounting points on the
bezel you have is the better path** — and the one that keeps an original piece in the car. Replace
only if it is cracked through the visible face.

Established repair methods for broken bezel tabs and bosses:

| Method | Use when |
|---|---|
| **Two-part epoxy** to re-bond the broken tab | A clean break with the piece still in hand. Weaker than original — reinforce it |
| **ABS strip backing**, cut to fit the moulded channel behind the boss, super-glue gel, clamped | The standard reinforcement. Rebuilds strength rather than just re-sticking |
| **J-nut** over a partially remaining tab | Some tab left to grip |
| **PVC board softened with a heat gun**, moulded to shape, bonded with JB Weld, screw into a J-nut | The boss is gone entirely and needs rebuilding from scratch |

Common thread in all of them: **get a metal fastener into the repair**. The original design has the
screw threading into unsupported plastic with no metal backing, which is why it failed in the first
place. A J-nut or threaded insert in the rebuild stops it happening again.

Plastic-repair shops do this work too — **Fibrenew** (already noted in
[`door-job-plan.md`](door-job-plan.md) for the door panels) specifically lists rigid plastic and
dashboard repair, and they are mobile.

No reproduction of this bezel was found — this is a used or NOS hunt.

| **ARPAC / Kijiji / Barrie flea market** | Same used-parts channels as the door trim — see [`parts-sourcing.md`](parts-sourcing.md) |

**Not a fix:** the Rvinyl-style woodgrain dash overlay kits are adhesive skins that go over existing
trim. They change appearance only and do nothing for a bezel that will not stay mounted.

## Screws

If the bosses are sound and screws are simply missing, match what is already in the other holes
rather than guessing — GM used trim screws of a specific length here, and running a longer screw
into a good boss is a fast way to split it and turn a $5 job into the hunt above.
